Mathematik: Fachwissenschaftliche Dokumentation

Zusammenfassung der prüfungsrelevanten mathematischen Sätze, Definitionen und Formeln.

1. Lineare & Quadratische Funktionen

Lineare Funktionenschar: Funktionsgleichung: f(x) = m * x + b
wobei m die Steigung und b den y-Achsenabschnitt darstellt.
Steigungsberechnung: m = (y2 - y1) / (x2 - x1)
Quadratische Funktionen: Normalform: f(x) = x² + p*x + q
Nullstellenbestimmung via p-q-Formel:
x1,2 = -(p/2) ± √((p/2)² - q)

2. Trigonometrie & Sätze

Satzgruppe des Pythagoras: Im rechtwinkligen Dreieck gilt:
a² + b² = c²
Gilt ausschließlich im rechtwinkligen Dreieck (Winkel = 90°).
Winkelbeziehungen (Trigonometrie): sin(α) = Gegenkathete / Hypotenuse cos(α) = Ankathete / Hypotenuse tan(α) = Gegenkathete / Ankathete

3. Stochastik & Wahrscheinlichkeit

Laplace-Experimente: Wahrscheinlichkeit bei gleichen Eintrittschancen:
P(E) = günstige Ergebnisse / mögliche Ergebnisse
Baumdiagramme:
  • Pfadregel 1: Entlang eines Pfades multiplizieren.
  • Pfadregel 2: Ergebnisse verschiedener Pfade addieren.

4. Prozentwert & Zinsstruktur

Prozent-Grundformel: W = G * (p / 100)
W = Prozentwert, G = Grundwert, p = Prozentsatz.
Zinseszinsformel: Kapital nach n Jahren:
Kn = K0 * (1 + p/100)^n

Englisch: Kompetenzbereiche & Methodenlehre

Strukturierte Hilfestellungen zur Optimierung schriftlicher Textproduktionen und formaler Grammatik.

Methodische Textproduktion (Writing)

Stellungnahme verfassen (Comment):

Ein strukturierter Aufsatz folgt einer dreiteiligen Gliederung:

  • 1. Introduction (Einleitung): Definition des Streitpunktes, These formulieren.
    "Nowadays, the controversy surrounding... is a widely discussed issue."
  • 2. Main Body (Hauptteil): Kontra-Argumente zuerst nennen, abschließend eigene (starke) Argumente ausführen.
  • 3. Conclusion (Fazit): Zusammenfassung der logischen Ergebnisse ohne neue Argumente.
    "In conclusion, weighing the pros and cons..."
Satzverknüpfer (Linking Words):

Ergänzend:

Furthermore, ... (Darüber hinaus)

In addition, ... (Zusätzlich)

Kontrastierend:

However, ... (Jedoch)

On the other hand, ... (Andererseits)

Grammatikalischer Fokus

Conditionals (Bedingungssätze):
  • Type I (Realistisch): If + Simple Present || Will-Future
    "If it rains, we will stay home."
  • Type II (Hypothetisch): If + Simple Past || Would + Infinitiv
    "If I had money, I would buy a car."
  • Type III (Unmöglich): If + Past Perfect || Would have + Past Participle
    "If we had studied, we would have passed."

The Rise of Smart Cities: A Path to the Future?

Today, more than half of the world's population lives in urban areas, and that number is expected to grow significantly over the next few decades. To manage the immense pressures of rapid urbanization, metropolitan planners are increasingly turning to technology to construct what are known as "Smart Cities". By integrating the Internet of Things (IoT), artificial intelligence, and cutting-edge sensor technology, these smart environments promise to optimize public resources, reduce carbon footprints, and make daily life much more convenient for their inhabitants.

One prime example of a functioning smart city system is intelligent traffic routing. Sensors placed along major city streets can detect real-time traffic jams and automatically adjust the duration of traffic light cycles. This keeps cars moving efficiently, reducing emissions caused by idling engines, and saving commuters valuable time. In some Scandinavian cities, trash containers are equipped with ultrasonic level sensors. When a bin is nearly full, it sends a digital notification directly to the local waste collection department. The collection trucks then only drive to the bins that actually need emptying, dramatically reducing fuel consumption.

However, critics of the smart city transformation argue that there are major concerns regarding digital privacy and massive surveillance. With thousands of cameras, sensors, and continuous data collection networks operating simultaneously, it is almost impossible for an individual to move through public spaces completely anonymously. Many people worry that this valuable private information could be compromised by cyber-criminals or used by governments to monitor and restrict citizen behaviors. Additionally, high-tech infrastructure requires incredibly expensive financial investments, which might lead to higher taxes for local citizens.
